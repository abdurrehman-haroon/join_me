Campus Pickup

A working iOS prototype. It covers the seven-week v1 plus the next set of features, drawn from how Yale, Princeton, Duke and Harvard run house sport. Everything on the phone can be tapped.

v1: the seven-week build

Open the Sports Complex game and tap Join game. Two seconds later someone else joins while you watch, and a push notification arrives.

Next: what we build once games are happening

  • Game day runs a game from arrival to result. You check in at the ground, split into Orange and Green bibs, and flip a 3D Rs 5 coin for the toss. Then you score it ball by ball. The live score shows on the map pin for the whole campus, and full time gives you a match card to share to your story. Score a few balls, then go back to the map to see the pin change.
  • Your games is where you say when you're free and what you play. A game that's one or two short can ping you. Regulars are standing weekly games where the core group gets first pick of spots before the game opens to campus.
  • Hostel Cup is a semester-long points table between hostels, with a 3D podium you can turn. Points come mostly from turning up rather than winning. Finish a game in Game day and your hostel's points go up here.
  • Player card is a tilting card showing games played, games turned up to and Cup points. Players will want to share it.
  • New campuses shows the waitlist for each campus. A campus opens only when enough students have signed up, so nobody lands on an empty map.
  • On the map, the conditions strip shows prayer times and air quality. Tap it for details.

Nothing here moves into the seven weeks

The plan's point still stands: a game that got organised in the app has to actually get played before anything else matters. Each phase below starts only when the one before it has hit its target, and the first target is the plan's own go/no-go after four weeks on campus.

Later: how it could make money

The pickup map itself stays free. Pickup apps that earn money tend to charge organisations, not players. GameTime moved from a free game finder to paid club tools, and Stanford runs its own court booking. The likely route is booking grounds with the sports office, then friendlies between campuses (LUMS vs NUST) and sponsored kit. Ads on an empty map don't work.

Safety still holds

Every new feature keeps the plan's four rules. Check-in confirms you're at a ground and then drops the location. Live scores show team colours, not players. Cup points come from an append-only ledger built from check-ins, so nobody can edit them by hand. Campus-email names stay in chat. Yik Yak's anonymous model grew to 200 campuses and then collapsed under bullying, which is exactly what verified names avoid.

How top US universities run house sport

  • Yale's Tyng Cup has run between residential colleges since 1933. Each game is worth points equal to the number of players on the field, so an 11-a-side game counts for more than 5-a-side. Yale Intramurals
  • Princeton's Campus Rec Cup gives points for leagues, fitness events and classes, plus a 50-point bonus for the group with the most different participants, however they did. That's where our "most different players" bonus comes from. Princeton Campus Rec
  • Duke's Quad Cup (2024) gives points just for attending and extra for forming a team. Duke Chronicle
  • Harvard's Straus Cup is between the 12 upperclass Houses. First-years compete separately for the Yard trophy, in everything from soccer to a spelling bee. The Crimson
  • IMLeagues runs intramurals at most US schools. It rates sportsmanship from 1 to 4, and teams averaging under 3 miss the playoffs. A team that forfeits pays a fee. A team that gives notice before a cutoff (a "default") doesn't. IMLeagues policy
  • Stanford has its own Rec & Well app for sign-ups, schedules and push notifications, plus separate court booking. Schools want their own tools, and that's a way in. App Store

I couldn't confirm MIT's or Brown's house competition rules, or women's hours at these schools, so neither is used above.

Apps that already do part of this

  • GotNext, GOATS and ATH for pickup basketball share one loop: pick a court, see who's checked in, tap "on my way". GotNext's pitch is to stop texting the group chat, which is our pitch too. GotNext
  • Plei for pickup soccer has 500,000 users across 26 US regions, so there's clearly demand for football specifically. Plei
  • Spond has 1.5 million monthly users. It puts chat, scheduling, attendance and reminders in one free app, which matches our roster, chat and reminders. Spond

How campus apps opened one school at a time

  • Facebook in 2004 held each school back until its waitlist reached about a fifth of the student body, then opened it with real density on day one. It went from Harvard to 15 schools in under three months. Yahoo Finance
  • Fizz recruited a few launch leaders per school. Each one recruited about ten more, and they handed out merch until sign-ups took off. It was adding about 50 campuses a month with no paid ads. Stanford case study
  • Yik Yak used the same one-campus model and reached 200 campuses in seven months. Anonymity made the bullying unmanageable, and it sold for $1M within four years. EM360

We use 10% of students rather than Facebook's 20%. A pickup game only needs density among people who play, and on top of the waitlist we require two campus leads who are already running games.

What's specific to Pakistan

  • Smog builds from late October and peaks in November and December. Some parts of Lahore passed AQI 1,000 in November 2024. For about three months, an AQI warning at kick-off matters. Air pollution in Lahore
  • Ramadan doesn't stop play. LUMS ran a tape-ball league in March 2025 scheduled around iftar. LUMS news
  • SLUMS (Sports at LUMS) runs more than 25 teams and 30 events a year. It's the natural partner for the pilot and the source of campus leads.
  • Campus sizes: about 5,500 students at LUMS, 14,900 at NUST, 4,000 at IBA Karachi, and roughly 5,500 at FAST Lahore. Sources for FAST disagree, so treat that one as a rough figure.

Direction: Floodlight

Pickup games on campus happen at dusk and after dark, under floodlights. The design is built from what's actually on the ground at 7pm: mown turf, chalk lines, a tennis ball wrapped in red electrical tape, and lights coming on over the pitch.

  • The number is the headline."4 spots left" is set at 120pt in stadium type. It's the one thing a player needs to decide whether to go, so nothing else on the screen competes with it.
  • The campus is a place.The map is a tilted model of the real grounds, lit for the hour you're looking at. Drag the time to 9pm and the floodlights and hostel windows come on.
  • The controls stay quiet.Controls use chalk and ink only. Colour comes from the pitch: turf, red tape, floodlight yellow. There are no gradients on buttons, no frosted cards, and no emoji icons.
  • Every action gets a response.When you join, you drop into a slot on the formation, the count rolls down, and a haptic fires. Someone else joining later shows up live on the same view.

What we took from the first research pass

The four sites you sent are all web-only. threeui.com (Three.js effects), GSAP (a web animation engine) and 21st.dev (React and shadcn components) can't run in a React Native app. We use them as references for motion and timing, then rebuild that motion with Reanimated and Skia. tasteskill.dev is a set of anti-generic design rules for coding agents. That one does apply here, and it's worth using as a review checklist on every screen.

What we're taking from other apps: Apple product pages tie motion to something you control, like scroll position or a slider, instead of playing it on their own. That's why the time slider drives the light here. Apple's Activity rings make one number the hero and celebrate when it changes, which is the model for the spots-left count. Partiful shows avatars popping in as guests RSVP, which is the model for the formation filling up. Airbnb's 2025 icons are tactile and 3D and react when you tap them, which is the model for our ball icons.

What we're avoiding: purple gradients, frosted-glass cards laid over the map, three identical cards in a row, emoji as icons, Inter everywhere, and bouncy easing on everything. The one blurred surface in the prototype is the iOS notification banner, and it's blurred because that's how iOS draws it.
